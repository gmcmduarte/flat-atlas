# Risks

Likelihood (L) and impact (I): high / medium / low. Reviewed at every gate.

| ID | Risk | L | I | Mitigation | Early warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **Data gaps.** Pre-1990 literature is thin in open sources. Abstracts are missing for much closed-access content: OpenAlex removed Springer non-OA abstracts (2022) and Elsevier coverage dropped (2024). The pilot venues are all Springer LNCS. | high | high | Tagger designed to work on titles + citations + embeddings; abstracts taken from the first of OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref; coverage measured in P1-02 and shown on the site; Phase 6 adds zbMATH Open and reference mining; missing classics can be added through suggestions. | `reports/coverage-pilot.md`: abstract share under 30 %; anchors failing for lack of text |
| **R2** | **API limits and source changes.** OpenAlex moved to keys and a daily budget in 2026; Crossref changed limits twice in 8 months; Semantic Scholar keys are granted by request and limited to 1 request/s; any source can change again or disappear. | high | medium | All access through one module with cache, back-off and budget guard; raw responses kept, so parsing never needs the network; limits in config, re-verified when first used; bulk dumps as the alternative path (DBLP, OpenAlex snapshot, S2 datasets are all free); each signal is optional in the tagger, so losing one source degrades quality without stopping the pipeline. | 429s in logs; budget guard stopping early; schema errors in parsers |
| **R3** | **Licensing.** ODC-BY (Semantic Scholar) and CC BY-SA (zbMATH) attached to parts of the data; abstracts may be copyrighted; CORE ranks have no stated licence; MSC2020 is CC BY-NC-SA; CCS is ACM copyright. | medium | high | Never redistribute abstracts; CC BY 4.0 for the dataset with attributions; zbMATH-derived fields in a separate share-alike file; own topic wording, codes only as cross-references; CORE ranks shown with link but not in the dataset; an automated licence check before every release; ask the university library or legal office before the first Zenodo release. | a source's terms page changes; a takedown request |
| **R4** | **Tagging quality.** Without labels and with few abstracts, tags may be noisy; fine-grained sibling topics may be inseparable; errors reduce trust in the whole Atlas. | high | high | Confidence and provenance shown everywhere; conservative thresholds (fewer, surer tags); evaluation suite with hard thresholds; anchors; spot-check reading at each gate by the domain expert; multi-signal agreement; taxonomy kept shallow where signals cannot separate topics; correction channel from day one. | anchors below 90 %; agreement under 0.2 for many topics; your own spot checks |
| **R5** | **Author disambiguation.** Homonyms, name changes, transliteration, authors outside DBLP; wrong merges are damaging for chair tools. | medium | high | DBLP PIDs as anchors; never merge two PIDs; other IDs mapped by shared papers; provisional authors flagged; chair tools use only confidence ≥ 0.8; evidence shown with every suggestion; author corrections through suggestions (and pointing people to DBLP's own correction process). | authors with disjoint topic/coauthor clusters; complaints |
| **R6** | **Scale.** The laptop has 22 GB free disk and 16 GB RAM; the field may be larger than estimated; the citation neighbourhood can explode. | medium | medium | Pilot under 5 GB; API-first design needs no snapshot; stubs keep only minimal fields; expansion limited to two rounds with a drift alarm; streaming parsers; DuckDB memory limit; measured bytes-per-work at gate G2 to confirm server sizing. | `data/` above 12 GB on the Mac; stub count above 10× in-scope works |
| **R7** | **Dependence on the university server.** It may arrive late, be smaller, forbid public ports or Docker, or be withdrawn. | medium | high | Request sent early (P3-00) with minimum and comfortable sizes; Phase 2 and most of Phase 3 run on the Mac; the static site scales to a reduced mirror of the full field (shards; top papers per topic); if no server: mass import on the laptop with an external disk, in venue batches, and features P5-01 to P5-03 in static variants; if no public ports: the server runs the pipeline and pushes static exports to GitHub Pages; containers make a move to another host a deployment. | no answer from IT after 4 weeks; restrictive firewall policy |
| **R8** | **Limited time of a solo maintainer.** The roadmap has 39 sessions plus reviews; attention is the scarce resource; long pauses lose context. | high | high | Small sessions with written hand-offs; `PROGRESS.md` makes resuming cheap; a usable public result after Phase 1 and after every later phase; strict parking lot; automation of refreshes; maintenance budget defined at G6 (target under 2 hours per month); features in Phase 5 are independent and can be dropped or reordered. | more than 3 weeks without a merged session; parking lot growing faster than the roadmap shrinks |
| **R9** | **Scope drift.** Verification and combinatorics literatures leak in through citations and keywords; or the rule is too strict and loses real FLAT work in broad venues. | medium | medium | Field guards; "no single weak signal" rule; round limit; hold-out recall and background false-positive tests; borderline status kept out of the site; scope reason stored per work; your 20-minute borderline read in P2-02 and at gates. | level-0 topic shares shifting sharply after an expansion round |
| **R10** | **Taxonomy bottleneck or drift.** The review is skipped or rushed; automatic proposals pile up; topics become inconsistent as the corpus grows. | medium | high | Review guide listing the 10 least certain decisions; reviewed fields are protected by tooling; proposals are batched (v1, v1.1, v2) with short review notes; `taxonomy check` in CI; yearly proposal review in maintenance. | untouched proposals older than one phase |
| **R11** | **Secrets and security.** Keys pushed to a public repo; a public server with an admin page and a write endpoint. | medium | high | `.env` git-ignored; gitleaks in pre-commit and CI; GitHub push protection; keys never pasted in prompts; read-only API; one write endpoint with validation, rate limit and honeypot; admin behind authentication; security pass at gate G3; minimal open ports; regular image updates. | gitleaks alert; unusual traffic in logs |
| **R12** | **People data and misuse.** The Atlas profiles authors (topics, co-authors, affiliations). Chair tools could be read as rankings of people; GDPR applies to personal data even if public. | medium | medium | Only public bibliographic facts; no composite person scores; clear notices; a contact route for corrections and objections; no tracking, no accounts, no stored user input; ask the university's data-protection officer before the server launch; removal/correction procedure documented. | a complaint; a request for erasure |
| **R13** | **Dependence on AI tooling.** Model or plan changes; a model confidently writes wrong facts (venue years, API limits) or subtly wrong transforms; large unreviewed diffs accumulate. | medium | medium | Facts verified against sources and dated; tests with real fixtures for every transform; evidence-based "done"; independent gate reviews; the plan and prompts live in the repo and work with any capable model; nothing in the product needs a paid model at run time. | tests added after the fact; you stop reading diffs |
| **R14** | **Bus factor and longevity.** One person, one server. | medium | medium | Public repo, open licences, Zenodo releases, runbooks, reproducible pipeline, static mirror on GitHub Pages; anyone can rebuild the Atlas from a release. | — |
